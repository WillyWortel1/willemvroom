FAQ
What is DMSO?

DMSO stands for dimethyl sulfoxide. It is a colourless solvent that comes from the paper and wood-pulp industry as a by-product. It was first made in the 19th century (1866) and is known for passing through the skin very easily.

What is DMSO said to do?

It has been researched for anti-inflammatory and pain-relieving effects, and as a 'carrier' that takes other substances through the skin. The evidence is mixed: some results are promising, but many studies are small or old. Many claims you read online have not been well tested.

Is DMSO an approved medicine?

In the United States DMSO is approved for one use only: a bladder instillation for interstitial cystitis, given by a doctor. In the EU and the Netherlands it is not approved as a medicine for home use. Products sold separately are therefore not controlled the way medicines are.

Why does it cause a garlic or oyster smell?

The body turns part of the DMSO into dimethyl sulfide. That substance leaves the body through breath and skin and smells of garlic or oysters. It is unpleasant but temporary.

What side effects are known?

The best known are skin irritation (redness, itching, dry or burning skin), the smell, headache and nausea. Because DMSO carries other substances through the skin, it can also change how medicines work.

What should I watch out for?

Purity and grade matter: DMSO also carries dirt and contaminants into the body. Use it only on clean skin without creams or other products, and test a small spot first. Take extra care in pregnancy, breastfeeding and with children, and don't casually mix it with other substances. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist, especially if you take medication.
